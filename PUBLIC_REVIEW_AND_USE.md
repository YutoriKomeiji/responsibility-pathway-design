# Public Review, Use, and Adaptation Posture

Responsibility Pathway Design (RPD) is a developing, reviewable design framework. Its provisional research status is a statement about evidence maturity, not a request that others avoid reading, applying, critiquing, or adapting it.

RPD uses a scoped licensing model: documentation, design, research text, templates, diagrams, and other non-software expressive material are CC BY 4.0 by default; software and executable scripts explicitly identified as software use the MIT License. See [`LICENSING.md`](LICENSING.md).

Readers may share and adapt the design material with attribution. The project welcomes real design use because application, counterexamples, failed mappings, ambiguous concepts, and competing designs are part of how the framework can improve.

## What is encouraged

- use RPD concepts in bounded design reviews;
- adapt templates and patterns to concrete cases;
- compare RPD with established safety, requirements, assurance, governance, and human-factors methods;
- report cases where a concept is too broad, too narrow, impractical, or misleading;
- submit counterexamples and failure cases;
- propose terminology corrections and simpler formulations;
- report where the design imposes excessive implementation or review burden;
- identify conditions under which an intervention helps, fails, or causes new harm;
- contribute empirical, operational, or affected-party evidence that supports revision.

## What provisional means

`Provisional` means the framework may change as external review and evidence accumulate.

It does **not** mean:

- the material is forbidden to use;
- every application requires waiting for a future certification;
- critique should be postponed until the framework is 'finished';
- successful or failed real-world use should remain outside the evidence loop.

## What still requires care

RPD does not create legal, institutional, moral, or operational authority. A design artifact should not be presented as certification, legal interpretation, safety proof, or authorization merely because it follows an RPD template.

Users should preserve the distinction between:

- descriptive analysis and normative judgment;
- source authority and interpretation;
- design verification and implementation verification;
- exercise evidence and operational evidence;
- rollback and wider responsibility recovery;
- assurance argument and final authorization.

## Contribution philosophy

RPD should improve through observable criticism and repair, not through isolation from use.

A strong contribution may be a successful pattern, but it may also be:

- a counterexample;
- a design that is too expensive to operate;
- a human gate that becomes rubber-stamping;
- a theoretically reversible action that is socially irreversible;
- a responsibility route that cannot be exercised under time pressure;
- a case where a control transfers risk to an affected party;
- evidence that a simpler design works better.

The objective is not to protect the framework from failure. The objective is to make failure visible enough to revise the framework responsibly.
